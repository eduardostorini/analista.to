What is TLS-RPT?

TLS-RPT (SMTP TLS Reporting, RFC 8460) is a companion mechanism to MTA-STS and DANE that gives a domain owner visibility into failures that occur when other mail servers try to negotiate a secure TLS connection to deliver mail. Without TLS-RPT, a domain that enforces a strict transport policy has no way of knowing when a legitimate sending server fails to connect — the sender's own logs may show the failure, but the receiving domain's operators are otherwise blind to it, which makes rolling out strict transport policies risky without a feedback loop. TLS-RPT is published as a DNS TXT record at _smtp._tls.<domain>, starting with v=TLSRPTv1, and its single meaningful tag is rua=, a comma-separated list of one or more URIs (typically a mailto: address or an https: endpoint) where cooperating senders periodically deliver aggregated JSON reports describing every TLS negotiation attempt against the domain's mail servers, successful or not.

How to interpret the result

If no TLS-RPT record exists, the domain will not receive any reports about failed TLS connections to its mail servers — this is a gap worth closing before or alongside enabling MTA-STS enforcement, since it removes the only visibility a domain owner has into whether a strict transport policy is actually breaking legitimate mail delivery. If a record exists but has no rua= tag, it is syntactically present but functionally useless, since senders have nowhere to deliver reports to. A properly configured record lists at least one reachable destination — most commonly a dedicated mailbox or a third-party TLS reporting service endpoint, since the raw JSON reports are not meant to be read manually at any meaningful scale.

Common use cases

This tool is used before and during an MTA-STS rollout, to confirm reporting is in place so that switching from testing to enforce mode does not happen blind. It is also used when auditing a domain's overall mail transport security posture, and to diagnose bounce or delay complaints from partner organizations that may be failing TLS negotiation against the domain's mail servers for reasons that would otherwise only surface in aggregate reports.

Limitations

This tool only confirms that a syntactically valid TLS-RPT record exists and where reports are supposed to be sent — it cannot fetch or interpret the aggregate reports themselves, which are delivered directly by cooperating senders (like Gmail and Outlook) to the configured rua= address on their own schedule, typically daily. Reviewing the actual content of those reports requires either a mailbox capable of parsing the gzipped JSON attachments or a dedicated TLS reporting service subscribed to the same address.

TLS-RPT complements MTA-STS and DANE

TLS-RPT does not enforce anything on its own — it is purely a reporting mechanism, and its value comes entirely from being paired with a transport security policy that can actually fail, namely MTA-STS or DANE (DNSSEC-based TLSA records). A domain can technically publish TLS-RPT without either of those in place, but the reports would then only ever describe plain opportunistic TLS negotiation issues, which is far less actionable. The recommended approach is to configure TLS-RPT first, confirm reports are flowing to a monitored address, then deploy MTA-STS starting in testing mode, and finally move to enforce once the reports show no unexpected legitimate failures — use the MTA-STS Checker to track that rollout alongside this tool.
