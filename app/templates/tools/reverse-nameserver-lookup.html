What is Reverse Nameserver Lookup?

Every domain on the internet needs nameservers to translate its name into an IP address. Those nameservers are often provided by a hosting company, a DNS provider, or a corporate IT department. When multiple domains use the same nameservers, they are frequently related: they may belong to the same organization, be hosted on the same infrastructure, or share a common DNS provider. Reverse Nameserver Lookup takes a domain and discovers which other domains are hosted on the same nameservers, giving you a quick view of related sites without needing access to private WHOIS or registrar data. This is useful for competitive research, infrastructure mapping, fraud investigation, and SEO analysis.

How to interpret the result

The result lists the nameservers returned by the domain's NS records, followed by other domains discovered on those nameservers. A long list of related domains usually indicates a shared hosting provider, a reseller account, or a large organization with many properties. A short or empty list can mean the domain uses private nameservers, a dedicated DNS service with few clients, or that the reverse lookup source simply did not index many domains on those nameservers. The related domains are not guaranteed to belong to the same owner; they only share DNS infrastructure. Some domains may be parked, expired, or inactive. For a more accurate ownership picture, combine this tool with WHOIS/RDAP lookup and reverse IP lookup.

Common use cases

Security analysts use reverse nameserver lookup during investigations to identify clusters of domains that may be operated by the same threat actor or phishing campaign. If several suspicious domains point to the same nameservers, that pattern can link them together even when WHOIS privacy hides the registrant. SEO professionals use it to find competitor domains that are hosted on the same infrastructure, which can reveal hosting strategies, shared hosting environments, or content farms. Digital forensics teams use it to map the footprint of a brand or individual across multiple websites without relying on public declarations of ownership. Support teams use it to answer questions like "which other sites are hosted on my nameservers?" or "is my domain sharing DNS with known bad actors?". It is also useful in journalism and research to uncover networks of related sites, political campaigns, or affiliate marketing operations that would otherwise remain hidden behind separate domain registrations.

Limitations

This tool relies on an external reverse nameserver index, which may not include every domain on the internet. Newly registered domains, recently migrated domains, or domains on low-traffic nameservers may be missing from the results. The list of related domains is based on nameserver overlap only, not on confirmed ownership; two unrelated organizations can use the same hosting provider and therefore share nameservers. Privacy-focused DNS providers, CDNs, and large cloud platforms often host thousands of unrelated domains on the same nameservers, so the result can be very large and less meaningful. Finally, this tool does not check whether the discovered domains are active, parked, or abandoned; it only reports the nameserver relationship. For a fuller picture, combine this tool with reverse IP lookup, WHOIS/RDAP, and historical DNS data sources.
