What is the WHOIS/RDAP query?

Every publicly registered domain has a set of administrative data held by the registry responsible for its extension (.com, .com.br, .io etc.): who registered it, which company (registrar) mediated the registration, when it was created, when it expires, and which nameservers are delegated to it. Historically, this data was queried via the WHOIS protocol, a simple text protocol with no standardized format across different registries. RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is the modern successor to WHOIS, with structured JSON responses and a more consistent format across different TLDs. This tool tries RDAP first — currently supported by most generic TLDs and a growing number of ccTLDs — and falls back to a traditional WHOIS query on port 43 when RDAP is not available for the queried domain's extension.

How to interpret the result

If the domain is not registered, the tool clearly indicates this — which means it is available for registration (subject to real-time availability at the registrar). When registered, the most important fields to watch are the expiration date (domains not renewed in time can enter a grace period and then become publicly available again, which represents a risk of brand hijacking) and the nameservers, which indicate which DNS provider the domain is effectively delegated to — useful for confirming whether a provider migration has been completed. The "status" field reflects administrative flags from the registry (such as locks against transfer or accidental deletion) defined by ICANN/registry/registrar.

Common use cases

Domain professionals use this tool to check if a name is available before attempting to register it, to confirm the expiration date of their own domains and avoid losing them through oversight, to identify the registrar responsible for a third-party domain in disputes or acquisition processes, and to quickly audit which nameservers a domain is using without needing to access the registrar's panel. Legal and security teams also resort to WHOIS/RDAP queries as part of investigations into the ownership of suspicious domains.

Limitations

Since privacy regulations such as the GDPR went into effect, most registrars hide personal data of the registrant (name, email, address) from public WHOIS/RDAP responses, displaying only the privacy service or registrar's own data — that is why this tool does not show personal contact information. Some country-specific TLDs still do not support RDAP and use proprietary WHOIS formats that this tool may not be able to fully interpret; in those cases, individual fields may appear empty even when the domain is registered.

Preventing accidental domain loss

One of the most valuable uses of this tool is preventive: periodically monitor the expiration date of domains that are important to your business. It is surprisingly common for a company to lose a valuable domain simply because the registrar's billing card expired, the renewal email landed in spam, or the person responsible for renewal left the company without passing on that responsibility. Once a domain expires and passes through the grace period without renewal, it returns to the market and can be registered by anyone — including domain speculators or competitors. Regularly checking the expiration date through this tool, independent of your registrar's panel, is an extra layer of protection against this kind of loss.
